Website Redesign Checklist: Strategy, Speed, SEO, and Launch

A redesign should improve how people understand and use your website. New visuals matter, but the strongest projects also clarify content, reduce friction, and protect the search value the existing site has earned.

Define what the new site must do

Start with the audience and its most important tasks. Identify which pages help visitors understand your offer, compare options, and make contact. Review analytics and customer questions to find where the current journey breaks down.

Set success measures before design begins. Useful measures might include qualified inquiries, completion of key tasks, page performance, or accessibility improvements.

Plan content and search together

Inventory existing pages and decide what to keep, improve, combine, or retire. Give every important page a clear purpose, descriptive title, helpful heading structure, and relevant internal links.

When URLs change, map old addresses to new ones before launch. Check metadata, canonical URLs, sitemap entries, and image descriptions so search engines and visitors can follow the new structure.

Test the real experience

Check the design on phones, tablets, and larger screens. Test navigation with a keyboard, confirm forms are usable, and make sure the page remains understandable when animations are reduced.

Measure loading performance on representative devices and connections. After launch, monitor errors, broken links, search indexing, and user feedback so the redesign keeps improving.

Prepare a launch checklist with owners

Assign responsibility for content approval, redirects, analytics, accessibility checks, form delivery, security, and final publishing. A launch plan is more useful when each check has a named owner and a clear pass condition.

Test the complete journey rather than isolated pages. Start from a search result, follow a service page, use the navigation, submit an inquiry, and confirm that the team receives it. Repeat on a phone and with a keyboard.

Keep a short monitoring window after release. Review errors and broken links daily at first, then adjust based on real visitor behavior. A redesign is complete when the new experience performs well for its audience, not merely when the files are online.
